\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related-work}

The living source atlas is intentionally broader than both the bibliography
shelf and the sources cited here. This section uses only promoted records and
organizes them by the question for which each source is informative. A study
may establish that an architecture existed while providing weak evidence for a
listener outcome; a theoretical framework may clarify measurement without
validating an implementation; and a normative or engineering precedent may
support a design transfer without becoming empirical affective evidence.

\subsection{Musical emotion, meaning, and response constructs}
\label{sec:related-musical-response}

Musical response is not a direct readout of an acoustic feature. Juslin and
V\"astfj\"all describe multiple candidate induction mechanisms---including
brain-stem reflexes, evaluative conditioning, emotional contagion, imagery,
episodic memory, and musical expectancy in addition to appraisal---whose
timing, volitional influence, cultural dependence, and relation to musical
structure differ \citep{juslin2008emotional}. The framework is theoretical,
but it makes a single acoustic-to-emotion rule an inadequate research model.
It also motivates retaining a person's context and interpretation rather than
treating a classifier output as the mechanism that operated.

Measurement work reinforces that distinction. Across four studies, Zentner
et al. derived and replicated the nine-factor Geneva Emotional Music Scale and
reported differences by genre and by whether emotion was perceived in the
music or felt by the listener \citep{zentner2008emotions}. This supports a
music-specific response vocabulary while also showing why no group-derived
taxonomy should replace personally meaningful language. AffectMachine-
Classical offers a complementary technical boundary: its 26-participant study
evaluated whether generated excerpts communicated intended affect, not whether
listeners felt that affect or benefited from it
\citep{agres2023affectmachine}. Expression alignment, felt response,
usefulness, and harm are therefore separate outcome questions.

An exclusively positive account of musical intensity would be incomplete.
Silverman et al.'s theoretical model treats music-induced harm as potentially
affective, behavioral, cognitive, identity-related, interpersonal, physical,
or spiritual, arising through interactions among context, deliverer, music,
and recipient \citep{silverman2020complicated}. The model does not estimate
risk or validate Antidote's safeguards. It does require the research record to
retain mismatch, unwelcome intensity, and harm for the person's interpretation
rather than recoding them as failed optimization or successful catharsis.

\subsection{Current-to-target and longitudinal personalization}
\label{sec:related-personalization}

Person- and moment-conditioned music predates Antidote. The personalized
affective music player combined current and desired affect, physiological
observations, and person-specific associations to select existing music over
multiple weeks \citep{janssen2012tune}. Its three-participant field evaluation
is too small to establish robust generalization, but it is direct precedent for
current-to-goal selection and longitudinal personal modeling.

The LUCID trial extended this design neighborhood to an explicit calm target,
the iso principle, preference information, and reinforcement-learning-based
sequencing of pre-existing tracks and nature sounds
\citep{mallik2022effects}. The open-label single-session study randomized 163
participants across music-plus-beats, music, beats, and pink-noise conditions;
some differences appeared in the moderate trait-anxiety subgroup and were less
differentiated in the high-anxiety subgroup. Commercial involvement, subgroup
dependence, and the inseparability of several design components prevent the
study from validating a universal journey or mechanism. BEAMERS likewise
documents strong person-dependent variation in response to the same song while
using EEG in a personalized recommendation setting \citep{li2022beamers}; as a
preprint, it is a comparator rather than an independent basis for an outcome
claim.

Together, these systems reject novelty claims based on personalization,
current-to-target sequencing, physiology, or longitudinal learning alone. They
also leave a methodological reason to model the unit of inquiry as a
person--moment--journey record rather than a fixed ``best music'' profile.

\subsection{Closed-loop and psychophysiological music systems}
\label{sec:related-closed-loop}

Physiological feedback has been connected to music at several levels of
complexity. iHeartLift used heart-rate-related feedback to manipulate music
tempo, demonstrating a narrow real-time loop rather than a semantic generator
\citep{ho2011iheartlift}. Daly et al.'s affective brain--computer music
interface combined EEG and other physiology, a desired affective trajectory,
algorithmic generation, and a person-specific model across sessions
\citep{daly2016abcmi}. Eight participants completed its longitudinal protocol;
attrition, constrained generation, and signal limitations qualify the result.
Ehrlich et al. reported continuous generated musical feedback with smooth
parameter control and user calibration, using an 11-listener perceptual study
and a five-participant BCI pilot \citep{ehrlich2019closedloop}. The preliminary
outcomes were heterogeneous and did not establish reliable regulation.

MindMelody is the closest recent semantic comparator. It connects real-time
EEG processing to global and local affect trajectories, a retrieval-augmented
semantic intervention plan, controllable MusicGen-based generation, and
physiological and subjective feedback \citep{zhang2026mindmelody}. Its reviewed
v2 preprint reports technical evaluations and a randomized within-subject
non-clinical pilot, but the pilot participant count was not found in the
visible methods. The system is strong prior art for an affect estimate to
semantic plan to generated music to response loop; its pilot is not Antidote
evidence and does not settle clinical efficacy.

Conflicting findings must remain in the same comparison. A 22-participant
preliminary minimalist brain--computer musical interface reported
nonsignificant target-emotion and time effects, with participant and trial
variation dominating the observed variance \citep{monroy2026minimalist}.
At field level, a systematic review found heterogeneous music-neurofeedback
methods and no consensus success measure \citep{sayal2025musicloop}. A separate
scoping review of commercial music-based digital therapeutics found product
presence but insufficient direct evidence for real-world effectiveness or
superiority over generic music \citep{venkatesan2026mdt}. Technical loop
closure, product availability, and beneficial response are therefore three
different evidence classes.

\subsection{Controllable generation and real-time architecture}
\label{sec:related-controllable-generation}

Interpretable generation controls also have established precedent.
AffectMachine-Classical maps continuous valence and arousal controls to a
rule-based and probabilistic generator that can change musical parameters bar
by bar \citep{agres2023affectmachine}; MindMelody exposes semantic plan fields
including tempo, texture density, section dynamics, and a target affect
trajectory \citep{zhang2026mindmelody}. Mustango adds explicit chord, beat,
tempo, and key conditioning to text-to-music generation and evaluates
condition adherence \citep{melechovsky2024mustango}. JASCO demonstrates global
text conditioning combined with local time-varying symbolic and audio
conditions \citep{tal2024jasco}. These systems support a translation from
human-readable intent into inspectable generator controls. They do not make
prompt mixtures perceptually linear, guarantee requested conditions in the
realized artifact, or establish felt-response control.

Antidote's timing and adaptation proposal therefore transfers engineering
ideas rather than outcome evidence. Adaptive game-music research supplies
precedents for transition-aware musical structure
\citep{hutchings2020adaptive}; just-in-time adaptive intervention design
separates decision points, tailoring variables, and intervention options
\citep{nahumshani2018jitai}; model-predictive control supplies receding-horizon
re-estimation \citep{garcia1989mpc}; and partially observable planning supplies
belief-state reasoning under uncertainty \citep{kaelbling1998pomdp}.
Interactive machine learning further motivates visible correction and override
instead of treating an estimate as authority \citep{amershi2014interactive}.
These transfers inform a two-rate, generate-ahead architecture. They do not
model a person as a controllable plant, validate an affect transition model,
define a therapeutic reward, or establish a stable real-time generator.

\subsection{Auditory beats: behavioral findings versus entrainment}
\label{sec:related-auditory-beats}

Auditory-beat outcomes and brainwave entrainment require separate claims. The
LUCID factorial comparison permits reporting subgroup-dependent behavioral
differences among music, beat, combined, and noise conditions
\citep{mallik2022effects}; it does not isolate a universal beat-frequency
mechanism. In contrast, Ingendoh et al.'s systematic review asked specifically
whether binaural-beat stimulation entrains EEG activity. Among 14 included
studies, five supported the entrainment hypothesis, eight contradicted it, and
one was mixed, with substantial heterogeneity in stimulation, design, EEG
measures, and analysis \citep{ingendoh2023binaural}. Binaural beats can thus be
treated as testable psychoacoustic controls, but the reviewed evidence does not
justify a rule that a frequency produces a known neural or therapeutic state.

\subsection{Music as setting and phenomenological variable}
\label{sec:related-setting}

Psychedelic research provides adjacent evidence that music can shape an
experience without being reducible to a treatment ingredient. In interviews
with 19 patients receiving psilocybin with psychological support for
treatment-resistant depression, Kaelen et al. identified welcome influences
such as emotion, imagery, guidance, openness, calm, and safety, alongside
unwelcome emotion, resistance, and misguidance
\citep{kaelen2018hidden}. Music-experience ratings were associated with acute
experience and later symptom change, but the small exploratory study had no
no-music comparison and cannot isolate music, playlist, preparation, support,
drug, or expectancy effects.

Rowe et al.'s scoping review included 19 papers from 10 studies and 330 total
participants \citep{rowe2026psychedelic}. It found preliminary relationships
with intensified emotion, meaning and imagery processes, and neural-dynamics
measures, while emphasizing small samples, methodological inconsistency, and
poorly standardized music conditions. The eligible evidence concerned LSD,
psilocybin, and ayahuasca rather than establishing a ketamine--music
comparison. This literature supports treating music as an active, temporally
structured, sometimes unwelcome setting variable. It does not validate
generated therapeutic music, an optimized soundtrack, or Antidote as a
psychedelic or ketamine intervention.

\subsection{Bounded contribution}
\label{sec:bounded-contribution}

Antidote therefore does not claim those components individually as new. It
formalizes a governed combination that preserves consented context,
user-extensible semantic intent, time-indexed planning, measured acoustic
realization, actual exposure, perceived and felt response, usefulness or harm,
aftereffect, provenance, and human-approved within-person updating as distinct
records. The bounded review did not identify that complete chain in one
evaluated system; this is a contribution rationale, not proof of global
novelty, learnability, or benefit.

The gap handed to System Design is consequently narrower than ``adaptive
music.'' Prior work establishes the ingredients and several close loops. The
remaining design question is how to keep the complete person-and-moment
transformation inspectable and human-correctable across context permission,
semantic intent, time-indexed planning, generated realization, actual
exposure, separated response layers, provenance, and any advisory update. Our
bounded review did not identify that complete governed chain in one evaluated
system. This statement supports the proposed combination boundary only; it is
not a global novelty, feasibility, or benefit claim.
