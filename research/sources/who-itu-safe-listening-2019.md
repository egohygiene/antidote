## Source claims assessed

- The standard treats listening risk as a function of sound level and exposure
  duration, represented through a sound-allowance or dosimetry function.
- It recommends an inspectable listening profile, cues for action, volume-
  limiting options, and accessible information about safe listening.
- The standard addresses personal audio devices and systems; it does not infer
  exposure at the ear from a digital file's loudness or peak measurement alone.

## Limitations and unresolved questions

- The standard does not validate Antidote's generated audio, protocol,
  headphones, device path, file-level targets, or future participants.
- A software player may not know calibrated output at the ear, environmental
  noise, other daily exposures, device fit, or individual susceptibility.
- A user-controlled volume slider and a stop control do not implement
  dosimetry, prevent cumulative exposure, or establish hearing safety.

## Allowed manuscript use

Cite to require level-and-duration accounting, accessible information, volume
control, and device-aware qualification before any human exposure, and to state
that file-level LUFS and true-peak checks are insufficient for hearing safety.

## Prohibited manuscript use

Do not convert the standard's reference modes into a universal Antidote dose,
claim that a file target or lowest-device-volume start is safe, or imply current
dosimetry, calibration, or conformance.
