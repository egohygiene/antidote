## Source claims assessed

- A JITAI is organized around distal and proximal outcomes, decision points,
  tailoring variables, intervention options, and decision rules.
- Tailoring variables may change over time, but should be selected because they
  are useful for a specified decision and proximal outcome.
- Availability, receptivity, burden, and unintended effects are part of the
  decision-rule design problem.

## Limitations and unresolved questions

- The source is a design framework, not evidence for any particular Antidote
  rule, outcome, sensor, or audio experience.
- Its examples concern mobile-health support rather than continuous music.
- Calling Antidote JITAI-inspired does not make it a validated intervention.

## Allowed manuscript use

Use as the primary health-adaptation anchor for decision points, tailoring
variables, intervention options, proximal outcomes, and explicit decision
rules. State that Antidote transfers these concepts into a proposed research
architecture.

## Prohibited manuscript use

Do not cite it as proof that moment-specific generated audio is effective,
clinically appropriate, optimally timed, or safe.
