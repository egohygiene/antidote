## Source claims assessed

- Four related studies characterized music-relevant emotion terms and
  introduced the nine-factor Geneva Emotional Music Scale (GEMS).
- Studies 1 and 2 included 354 listeners across groups with different music
  preferences; felt and perceived responses varied by genre and response type.
- A music-festival field study with 801 participants derived the nine-factor
  structure, and a fourth study with 238 participants replicated it.
- In the reported comparisons, the music-specific model accounted for
  music-elicited emotion better than the evaluated basic-emotion and
  dimensional alternatives.

## Limitations and unresolved questions

- A domain-specific scale does not make self-report objective, exhaustive, or
  interchangeable with acoustic features, physiology, usefulness, or harm.
- The studies do not validate a generated-audio controller or a
  person-specific longitudinal model.
- A group-derived factor structure may omit personally meaningful language.

## Allowed manuscript use

Cite as measurement evidence that perceived and felt musical emotion require
explicit distinction and that music-specific response vocabularies can add
resolution beyond a single valence--arousal label.

## Prohibited manuscript use

Do not treat GEMS factors as universal states, infer a listener's response from
the music alone, or claim that Antidote's semantic vocabulary is validated by
this scale.
