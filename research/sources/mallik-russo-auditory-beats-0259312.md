## Source claims assessed

- The open-label randomized single-session study enrolled 163 participants
  taking anxiolytic medication and compared music plus auditory beats, music,
  auditory beats, and pink noise.
- The LUCID condition used self-reported current mood, a calm target, the iso
  principle, preference data, and reinforcement learning to sequence
  pre-existing tracks and nature sounds.
- Some between-condition findings appeared in the moderate trait-anxiety
  subgroup; effects were less differentiated in the high-anxiety subgroup.

## Limitations and unresolved questions

- The study is open-label, single-session, and subgroup-dependent; it does not
  isolate a universal mechanism or long-term benefit.
- A coauthor disclosed a scientific/advisory and equity relationship with the
  company behind the system.
- It sequences curated audio rather than continuously generating or adapting
  audio within the session.

## Allowed manuscript use

Cite as a clinical-study comparator for current-to-target affective sequencing,
explicit mood input, preference-informed personalization, and auditory-beat
factorial comparison, with conflicts and subgroup limitations visible.

## Prohibited manuscript use

Do not attribute outcomes solely to music, auditory beats, personalization, or
the iso principle. Do not claim that current-to-target personalized sequencing
is unique to Antidote.
