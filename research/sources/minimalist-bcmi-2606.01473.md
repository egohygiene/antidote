## Source claims assessed

- The prototype maps two-channel frontal EEG estimates to stochastic musical
  controls including mode, tempo, rhythmic density, and register in real time.
- The preliminary evaluation included 22 participants.
- The reported target-emotion and time effects were nonsignificant, while
  participant and trial variation dominated the observed variance.

## Limitations and unresolved questions

- The study lacks no-music and yoked-feedback controls and uses a small,
  preliminary sample.
- Frontal alpha asymmetry is not an objective emotion label.
- The negative result qualifies assumptions that a technically closed loop will
  reliably produce user-controllable affective change.

## Allowed manuscript use

Cite as a recent direct comparator and important conflicting result for
real-time EEG-driven stochastic music generation.

## Prohibited manuscript use

Do not omit the null effects or present the system as proof that EEG-driven
music regulates emotion. Do not characterize technical loop closure as
behavioral efficacy.
