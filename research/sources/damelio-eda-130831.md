## Source claims assessed

- The review evaluates electrodermal-activity systems used for automatic
  affect or emotion recognition.
- The synthesis reports stronger performance for arousal-oriented inference
  than for valence-oriented inference in the reviewed literature.
- It identifies mismatches between continuous affect theory and common
  discrete classification tasks as a methodological concern.

## Limitations and unresolved questions

- Performance in curated datasets does not establish person-independent or
  moment-valid inference in natural listening settings.
- Labels, preprocessing, features, tasks, devices, and validation designs vary
  across the literature.
- The review does not evaluate Antidote or establish that EDA measures a
  person's need, meaning, safety, or benefit.

## Allowed manuscript use

Use as qualifying evidence that a future EDA adapter would require
person-specific validation, uncertainty, correction, and especially caution
for valence inference.

## Prohibited manuscript use

Do not describe EDA as an objective emotion decoder or claim that it reveals
the correct audio, intervention, diagnosis, or lived experience.
