## Source claims assessed

- The conceptual article defines music-induced harm as a multifaceted concern
  that may be affective, behavioral, cognitive, identity-related,
  interpersonal, physical, or spiritual.
- Its theoretical model treats harm as emerging from interactions among
  contextual, deliverer, music, and recipient factors.
- The model is intended to inform safer music practices and highlights the
  relevance of training, credentialing, regulation, education, and oversight.

## Limitations and unresolved questions

- The model is theoretical and does not estimate incidence, predict individual
  harm, or evaluate Antidote.
- Professional music-therapy governance does not transfer automatically to a
  self-directed generative-audio prototype.
- Enumerating harm categories is not proof that software controls mitigate
  them.

## Allowed manuscript use

Cite to reject an automatically positive account of musical intensity and to
justify retaining adverse, mismatched, identity-related, physical, and
spiritual responses for the person's interpretation.

## Prohibited manuscript use

Do not present the model as an empirical rate of harm, a validated risk score,
or evidence that consent, local execution, or a stop control makes Antidote
safe.
