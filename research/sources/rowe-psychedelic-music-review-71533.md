## Source claims assessed

- The PRISMA-guided scoping review included 19 papers from 10 studies with 330
  total participants in therapeutic and controlled experimental settings.
- Included evidence concerned LSD, psilocybin, and ayahuasca; the eligible set
  did not establish a ketamine--music comparison.
- The synthesis reports preliminary relationships with intensified emotion,
  meaning and imagery processes, and neural-dynamics measures.
- Music delivery and comparison conditions were incompletely and
  inconsistently reported, and only one included experiment used a fully
  factorial psychedelic-versus-placebo and music-versus-no-music design.

## Limitations and unresolved questions

- Small samples, open-label studies, heterogeneous methods, reused datasets,
  and indirect associations limit causal and clinical inference.
- The review does not establish an optimal music-delivery method, an
  independent therapeutic effect, or a generated-audio mechanism.
- Scoping-review coverage maps the field but does not pool one treatment
  effect.

## Allowed manuscript use

Cite as field-level evidence that music is studied as a consequential setting
variable in psychedelic research while delivery, mechanism, and independent
outcome contribution remain unresolved. Use the absence of an eligible
ketamine--music comparison only as a boundary of this reviewed set.

## Prohibited manuscript use

Do not claim that music independently improves psychedelic outcomes, that the
review validates personalization or generation, or that ketamine-specific
music effects were established.
