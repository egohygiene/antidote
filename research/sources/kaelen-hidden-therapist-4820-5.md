## Source claims assessed

- The study analyzed semi-structured interviews from 19 patients who received
  psilocybin with psychological support for treatment-resistant depression.
- Interpretative phenomenological analysis identified both welcome influences
  of music---including emotion, imagery, guidance, openness, calm, and
  safety---and unwelcome influences including unpleasant emotion, resistance,
  and a sense of misguidance.
- Ratings of liking, resonance, and openness were associated with acute
  experience measures; the quality of the music experience was reported as
  predictive of depression change one week later.

## Limitations and unresolved questions

- This small exploratory study had no no-music comparison and cannot isolate
  the causal contribution of music, playlist construction, preparation,
  psychological support, psilocybin, or expectancy.
- Qualitative coding and correlational prediction do not establish a general
  treatment mechanism or an optimal soundtrack.
- Amanda Feilding directed, and David Nutt advised, the Beckley Foundation,
  which was among the study sponsors.

## Allowed manuscript use

Cite as adjacent phenomenological evidence that music can be an active and
sometimes unwelcome setting variable, motivating explicit mismatch, harm,
stop, and person-interpretation records.

## Prohibited manuscript use

Do not claim that music caused the clinical outcome, that generated music would
reproduce the reported experience, or that Antidote is a psychedelic therapy.
