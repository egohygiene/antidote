## Source claims assessed

- The guidance places ethics and human rights within the design, deployment,
  and use of AI for health rather than treating them as a post-deployment
  addition.
- It identifies six consensus principles: protecting autonomy; promoting
  well-being, safety, and the public interest; ensuring transparency,
  explainability, and intelligibility; fostering responsibility and
  accountability; ensuring inclusiveness and equity; and promoting responsive
  and sustainable AI.
- The recommendations distribute accountability across developers, providers,
  institutions, governments, workers, and communities affected by health AI.

## Limitations and unresolved questions

- The guidance is normative; it does not evaluate Antidote or prove that a
  particular control achieves autonomy, safety, equity, or meaningful consent.
- Antidote is currently framed as a research instrument rather than a clinical
  system, but health-adjacent positioning still requires a conservative human
  rights and governance boundary.
- Applicable research review, law, accessibility, and data-protection duties
  depend on the eventual study, institution, jurisdiction, participants, and
  deployment context.

## Allowed manuscript use

Use as an authoritative governance anchor for human authority, transparency,
accountability, inclusiveness, safety review, and an explicit independent gate
before formal health-adjacent research collection or deployment.

## Prohibited manuscript use

Do not present alignment with the guidance as ethics approval, legal
compliance, clinical validation, safety evidence, or authorization to collect
human data.
