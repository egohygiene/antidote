## Source claims assessed

- Ecological momentary assessment repeatedly samples current experiences and
  behavior close to their occurrence in everyday settings.
- The approach can reduce some retrospective-recall problems and represent
  temporal and contextual variation.
- Reactivity, compliance, missingness, sampling strategy, burden, and analysis
  remain material methodological concerns.

## Limitations and unresolved questions

- Near-real-time self-report is not an objective or complete representation of
  inner state.
- The review does not validate Antidote's items, sampling windows, condition
  sequence, or later-aftereffect interval.
- Repeated prompting may itself change experience and willingness to continue.

## Allowed manuscript use

Use to motivate a session-proximal baseline, immediate response, declared later
window, explicit burden measure, and complete missingness accounting.

## Prohibited manuscript use

Do not claim that momentary assessment removes recall bias, establishes causal
effects, reveals true emotion, or makes a self-study generalizable.
