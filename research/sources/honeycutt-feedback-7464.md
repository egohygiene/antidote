## Source claims assessed

- The study used a simulated object-detection system to test how soliciting
  interactive feedback affected participants' impressions.
- In that setting, providing feedback reduced reported trust and impressions
  of system accuracy, including when feedback improved simulated performance.
- The result qualifies assumptions that more feedback or control necessarily
  improves a person's experience of an intelligent system.

## Limitations and unresolved questions

- The task involved image-based object detection rather than affective audio,
  sensitive context, or repeated within-person use.
- Perceived trust and accuracy do not directly measure burden, agency, safety,
  musical control, or health outcomes.
- The study does not determine the appropriate number or design of Antidote
  controls.

## Allowed manuscript use

Use as qualifying evidence that feedback requests can carry interaction and
trust costs and therefore require measurement rather than optimistic
assumption.

## Prohibited manuscript use

Do not claim that Antidote's controls will reduce trust, that feedback is
harmful in general, or that removing human review would improve the system.
